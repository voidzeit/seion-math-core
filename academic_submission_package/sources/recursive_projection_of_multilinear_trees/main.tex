\begin{abstract}
Let $T$ be a finite rooted ordered tree whose internal vertices carry bounded multilinear
maps between finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces, and suppose that the output at each
internal vertex is orthogonally projected onto a prescribed subspace of its ambient
space. We compare the value obtained by unprojected evaluation with the value obtained
by recursive projection. We derive an exact local decomposition of the discrepancy into a
closure residual created at the vertex and a sum over all nonempty subsets of the
children whose subtree errors interact, and we show that at the root the discrepancy
splits orthogonally, with the reduced-coordinate error equal to the projected component
exactly. Under uniform bounds $M$ on the multilinear maps and $\rho$ on their failure to
preserve the prescribed subspaces, we prove that a tree with $k\ge1$ internal vertices
satisfies $E_T^{\amb}\le k\rho M^{k-1}L_T$ while $E_T^{\proj}=E_T^{\red}\le(k-1)\rho
M^{k-1}L_T$, where $L_T$ is the product of the leaf norms; the reduction from $k$ to
$k-1$ is exactly the removal of the root closure residual by the final projection. We
also derive state-resolved and pathwise upper bounds, prove that a closed-form ordering
rule minimises the telescoping bound within a declared scalar family, and treat finite
signed linear combinations of trees. The coefficient $k-1$ is an upper bound; it is not
shown to be attained at fixed $\rho/M>0$. Over a registry of \VThreeGapRegistryRows{}
configurations, the constant is determined in \VThreeDeterminedPositive{} cases and
vanishes by the theorem in \VThreeZeroByTheorem{} further cases, while in
\VThreeNoPositiveLowerBound{} cases (\VThreeNoPositiveLowerPercent{}\,\%) no positive
lower bound was obtained at all. Determining the exact constant, already for two and
three internal vertices, remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Finite compositions of multilinear maps arise in nonassociative algebra, in the syntax of
coloured operads, in hierarchical tensor computations, and in projection-based model
reduction. In all of these settings a recurring computational step is to project the
output of every intermediate operation back onto a prescribed subspace, either because
the subspace carries the structure one wishes to preserve, or because storing the full
ambient object is too expensive.

Projection is exact only when the prescribed subspaces are invariant under the maps. When
they are not, each internal vertex creates a residual lying in the orthogonal complement
of its subspace, and these residuals then propagate through the remaining composition.
The question studied here is how those local residuals accumulate.

A crude answer is available immediately. Bounding every intermediate quantity by the
product of the operator norms yields an estimate that is valid but uninformative: it
neither identifies which vertex is responsible for the error, nor distinguishes the part
of the error that survives the final projection from the part that does not, nor exploits
the geometry of orthogonality at all. The purpose of this article is to replace that
estimate by a decomposition that does all three.

\subsection*{What is proved}

The starting point is an exact identity. At each internal vertex, the discrepancy between
the two evaluations is the sum of a locally created residual and a term for each nonempty
subset of the children whose subtree errors are simultaneously active
(Theorem~\ref{thm:subset}). No inequality is used, and no expansion is truncated. Applying
the vertex projector annihilates the locally created residual exactly and leaves the
propagated terms untouched.

From this identity three kinds of estimate follow. The first is a uniform estimate: with
$M$ bounding the multilinear maps and $\rho$ bounding their closure residuals,
\[
 E_T^{\amb}\le k\rho M^{k-1}L_T,
 \qquad
 E_T^{\proj}=E_T^{\red}\le(k-1)\rho M^{k-1}L_T
\]
for every tree with $k\ge1$ internal vertices (Theorem~\ref{thm:homogeneous}). The two
coefficients differ by exactly one, because the ambient error may contain a residual
created at every internal vertex, whereas the residual created at the root lies in the
orthogonal complement of the root subspace and is annihilated by the final projection.

The second is a family of vertexwise estimates that track, separately, the projected and
orthogonal components of every subtree error (Section~\ref{sec:state}). These are finer
than the uniform estimate because a residual created orthogonally at one vertex can
acquire a projected component at its parent, and the two situations carry different
constants.

The third attributes the bound to individual vertices: unrolling a valid scalar
recurrence expresses the estimate as a sum over internal vertices of a locally created
source multiplied by the gains along its path to the root (Section~\ref{sec:pathwise}).

\subsection*{What is not proved}

The coefficient $k-1$ is an upper bound. We do not show that it is the best possible
coefficient at a fixed positive value of $\eta=\rho/M$, and the numerical evidence
assembled here does not settle the question: explicit constructions attain the ambient
coefficient in the configurations where it is determined, while the projected
constructions we have found remain strictly below $k-1$ except in a small number of
configurations. Section~\ref{sec:lowcases} reports what is known for two and three
internal vertices; in both cases the exact constant is undetermined over most of the
parameter range.

Every result is finite-dimensional. Nothing here is a statement about infinite
composition, about continuum limits, or about function spaces.

\section{Relation to previous work}
\label{sec:priorwork}

The typed composition of multilinear operations is the setting of coloured operads
\cite{Yau2016,LodayVallette2012}, from which we borrow only the bookkeeping: the trees
here are finite and the operations are concrete linear-algebraic objects. Signed
combinations of composition trees are the natural home of the nonassociative identities
of Filippov \cite{Filippov1985} and of the Akivis--Sabinin circle of ideas
\cite{MostovoyPerezIzquierdoShestakov2014}.

The question of how a map that is only approximately structure-preserving behaves under
composition is classical in operator algebra: Johnson's work on approximately
multiplicative maps between Banach algebras \cite{Johnson1988} asks a related question
under global hypotheses, but does not address a fixed finite tree with prescribed
subspaces at every vertex.

Forward and backward error analysis has always treated the order of composition and the
propagation of local perturbations as central \cite{Higham2002}; the estimates of
Sections~\ref{sec:state}--\ref{sec:pathwise} are recognisably of that kind. Layerwise
Lipschitz estimates for composed operators \cite{CombettesPesquet2020} and sound abstract
interpretation for layered computations \cite{GehrEtAl2018} show, in different settings,
that a structure-aware summary can improve on a plain product of norms. We do not claim
that a vertexwise recursion is itself new.

Hierarchical tensor formats organise computation along a dimension tree and adapt the
approximation to it \cite{HackbuschKuhn2009,BallaniGrasedyck2014}; canonical polyadic
decomposition, factor nonuniqueness and identifiability are a separate matter
\cite{KoldaBader2009,Kruskal1977}. The dominant error in those formats is representation
or rank truncation. The dominant error here is different in kind: it is a closure defect
created at every algebraic vertex, and Section~\ref{sec:representation} keeps the two
apart deliberately. Projection-based structure-preserving model reduction supplies the
closest methodological precedent \cite{EggerEtAl2018}.

Interval arithmetic \cite{MooreKearfottCloud2009} and moment relaxations
\cite{Lasserre2001} provide rigorous global tools when their hypotheses and solver
statuses are verified. We use directed interval recursion and, in a few low-dimensional
cases, exact symbolic elimination. We do not report a semidefinite relaxation as a
certificate.

\section{Typed multilinear composition trees}
\label{sec:setting}

Let $\Types$ be a finite set of \emph{types}. To each $\tau\in\Types$ we associate a
finite-dimensional Hilbert space $V_\tau$ over $\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$, a
second finite-dimensional Hilbert space $W_\tau$, and an isometry
\[
 Q_\tau:W_\tau\longrightarrow V_\tau,
 \qquad
 Q_\tau^*Q_\tau=I_{W_\tau}.
\]
We call $V_\tau$ the \emph{ambient} space and $W_\tau$ the \emph{reduced} space of type
$\tau$, and we write
\[
 P_\tau=Q_\tau Q_\tau^*,
\]
so that $P_\tau$ is the orthogonal projector of $V_\tau$ onto $\ran Q_\tau$, and
$P_\tau^2=P_\tau=P_\tau^*$, $\norm{P_\tau}=1$.

Throughout, $\ran P_\tau$ is a fixed linear subspace of $V_\tau$. It is not a tangent
space, and we use the words \emph{projected} and \emph{orthogonal} for the two components
of a vector relative to it.

\begin{definition}[Composition tree]
\label{def:tree}
A \emph{typed composition tree} $T$ is a finite rooted ordered tree together with:
an output type $\tau(v)\in\Types$ and an arity $a_v\ge1$ for each internal vertex $v$;
input types $\tau(v,1),\ldots,\tau(v,a_v)$; a multilinear map
\[
 \mu_v:\prod_{j=1}^{a_v}V_{\tau(v,j)}\longrightarrow V_{\tau(v)};
\]
and a type $\tau(\ell)\in\Types$ for each leaf $\ell$. The $j$th child of $v$ must have
output type $\tau(v,j)$. Trees violating this condition are not admitted: they have no
numerical semantics.
\end{definition}

We write $\Int T$ for the set of internal vertices, $k(T)=\lvert\Int T\rvert$, and
$a_{\max}=\max_{v}a_v$. The multilinear operator norm is
\[
 \norm{\mu}_\op
 =\sup\{\norm{\mu(x_1,\ldots,x_a)}:\norm{x_1}=\cdots=\norm{x_a}=1\}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Closure-residual map]
\label{def:closure}
For an internal vertex $v$ with children $c_1,\ldots,c_{a}$, the \emph{closure-residual
map} is the multilinear map
\[
 r_v
 =(I-P_{\tau(v)})\,\mu_v\bigl(P_{\tau(c_1)}\,\cdot\,,\ldots,P_{\tau(c_a)}\,\cdot\,\bigr).
\]
It vanishes identically if and only if
$\mu_v(\ran P_{\tau(c_1)},\ldots,\ran P_{\tau(c_a)})\subseteq\ran P_{\tau(v)}$, that is,
if and only if the prescribed subspaces are invariant under $\mu_v$.
\end{definition}

We abbreviate $P_v=P_{\tau(v)}$ when no confusion arises.

\subsection{Standing hypotheses}
\label{sec:standing}

Every statement in this article is made under the following hypotheses, which are not
repeated in the individual statements.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(H\arabic*)]
\item\label{h:field} The scalar field is $\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$, fixed
throughout. Nothing below distinguishes the two cases.
\item\label{h:spaces} For each $\tau\in\Types$, $V_\tau$ and $W_\tau$ are
finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces over $\mathbb K$, with $1\le\dim W_\tau\le\dim V_\tau$.
\item\label{h:proj} $Q_\tau:W_\tau\to V_\tau$ is an isometry and $P_\tau=Q_\tau Q_\tau^{*}$
is the associated \emph{orthogonal} projector. Obliqueness is not permitted; see
Example~\ref{ex:oblique}.
\item\label{h:tree} $T$ is a finite rooted ordered tree. Every internal vertex has finite
arity $a_v\ge1$, and $\Types$ is finite.
\item\label{h:bounded} Every $\mu_v$ is multilinear and bounded, with
$\norm{\mu_v}_\op<\infty$. In finite dimension this is automatic and is stated for
emphasis.
\item\label{h:types} The tree is type-compatible in the sense of
Definition~\ref{def:tree}. Type-incompatible trees are not admitted and receive no
numerical semantics.
\item\label{h:leafdata} Leaf data $z_\ell$ lie in the \emph{reduced} spaces
$W_{\tau(\ell)}$, so that $F_\ell=R_\ell=Q_{\tau(\ell)}z_\ell\in\ran P_{\tau(\ell)}$.
\end{enumerate}

The three constants have the following exact meaning.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
$M$ & any real number with $\norm{\mu_v}_\op\le M$ for \emph{every} internal vertex $v$;
necessarily $M\ge0$ \\
$\rho$ & any real number with $\norm{r_v}_\op\le\rho$ for \emph{every} internal vertex $v$,
$r_v$ being the closure-residual map of Definition~\ref{def:closure}; necessarily
$\rho\ge0$ \\
$L_T$ & $\prod_{\ell}\norm{z_\ell}$, the product over the leaves of the norms of the
reduced leaf data \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Neither $M$ nor $\rho$ is required to be attained. Since $\norm{I-P_v}\le1$ and
$\norm{P_{\tau(c_i)}}\le1$, one has $\norm{r_v}_\op\le\norm{\mu_v}_\op$ at every vertex, so
$\rho$ may always be chosen with $\rho\le M$; the ratio $\eta=\rho/M$ of
Section~\ref{sec:lowcases} is understood with that choice and lies in $[0,1]$ when $M>0$.

\section{Unprojected and recursively projected evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluations}

Fix reduced leaf data $z_\ell\in W_{\tau(\ell)}$ and set
\[
 L_T=\prod_{\ell\in\mathrm{Leaves}(T)}\norm{z_\ell}.
\]

\begin{definition}[The two evaluations]
\label{def:evaluations}
The \emph{unprojected} (or ambient) evaluation is
\[
 F_\ell=Q_{\tau(\ell)}z_\ell,
 \qquad
 F_v=\mu_v(F_{c_1},\ldots,F_{c_{a_v}}),
\]
and the \emph{recursively projected} evaluation is
\[
 R_\ell=Q_{\tau(\ell)}z_\ell,
 \qquad
 R_v=P_{\tau(v)}\,\mu_v(R_{c_1},\ldots,R_{c_{a_v}}).
\]
The \emph{local discrepancy} at $v$ is $D_v=F_v-R_v$, with components
\[
 D_v^{\parallel}=P_vD_v,
 \qquad
 D_v^{\perp}=(I-P_v)D_v.
\]
\end{definition}

By construction $R_v\in\ran P_v$ for every internal $v$, and $R_\ell\in\ran P_{\tau(\ell)}$
at every leaf.

Write $\varrho$ for the root of $T$ and abbreviate $F=F_\varrho$, $R=R_\varrho$,
$P=P_{\tau(\varrho)}$, $Q=Q_{\tau(\varrho)}$. The four quantities of interest are
\[
 E_T^{\amb}=\norm{F-R},
 \quad
 E_T^{\proj}=\norm{PF-R},
 \quad
 E_T^{\perp}=\norm{(I-P)F},
 \quad
 E_T^{\red}=\norm{Q^*F-Q^*R}.
\]
The first is the discrepancy in the ambient space; the second is what remains of it after
the final projection; the third is what the final projection discards; the fourth is the
discrepancy as measured in the reduced coordinates, which is what a computation carried
out entirely in the reduced spaces would report.

\section{Exact local decomposition}
\label{sec:local}

\begin{theorem}[Exact local decomposition]
\label{thm:subset}
Let $v$ be an internal vertex of arity $a$ with children $c_1,\ldots,c_a$, and write
$F_i=F_{c_i}$, $R_i=R_{c_i}$, $D_i=F_i-R_i$. For $S\subseteq[a]$ put
\[
 y_i^S=\begin{cases}D_i,&i\in S,\\ R_i,&i\notin S.\end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
 D_v
 =r_v(R_1,\ldots,R_a)
 +\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[a]}\mu_v(y_1^S,\ldots,y_a^S).
\]
\end{theorem}

The identity is exact: no term is discarded and no inequality is used. The term indexed
by $S$ records precisely which children carry an error. Singletons are single-branch
propagation; subsets of size at least two are genuine interactions between the errors of
different branches.

Two consequences are used repeatedly. Since $P_vr_v=0$,
\[
 D_v^{\parallel}
 =\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[a]}P_v\mu_v(y_1^S,\ldots,y_a^S):
\]
\emph{the residual created at a vertex never appears in that vertex's own projected
error.} Conversely, $\mu_v$ need not map the orthogonal complement into itself, so a
child's orthogonal error can contribute to its parent's projected error,
\[
 P_v\mu_v(\ldots,D_i^{\perp},\ldots)\ne0
 \quad\text{in general.}
\]
Both effects are visible in the constants of Section~\ref{sec:uniform}.

\section{Orthogonal splitting at the root}
\label{sec:root}

\begin{proposition}[Root geometry]
\label{prop:root}
For every typed composition tree,
\[
 \bigl(E_T^{\amb}\bigr)^2
 =\bigl(E_T^{\proj}\bigr)^2+\bigl(E_T^{\perp}\bigr)^2,
 \qquad
 E_T^{\red}=E_T^{\proj}.
\]
\end{proposition}

Both are identities. The first is the Pythagorean theorem applied to
$F-R=(PF-R)+(I-P)F$, whose two summands lie in $\ran P$ and $(\ran P)^\perp$
respectively; the second says that a computation performed in the reduced coordinates
observes exactly the projected component of the ambient discrepancy, no more and no less.

Orthogonality of the projectors is used, and cannot be dropped.

\begin{example}[Failure for an oblique projector]
\label{ex:oblique}
Let $P=\begin{psmallmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}$ on $\mathbb R^2$. Then $P^2=P$, so
$P$ is a projector, but $P^*\ne P$. For $D=(0,1)^{\!\top}$ one has $PD=(1,0)^{\!\top}$ and
$(I-P)D=(-1,1)^{\!\top}$, whence $\langle PD,(I-P)D\rangle=-1\ne0$: the two components are
not orthogonal and Proposition~\ref{prop:root} fails.
\end{example}

\section{State-resolved estimates}
\label{sec:state}

Splitting each child discrepancy as $D_i=D_i^{\parallel}+D_i^{\perp}$ and substituting
into Theorem~\ref{thm:subset} expresses the value at $v$ as a sum over states
\[
 \mu_v(F_1,\ldots,F_a)
 =\sum_{\sigma\in\{R,\parallel,\perp\}^{a}}
 \mu_v\bigl(Z_1^{\sigma},\ldots,Z_a^{\sigma}\bigr),
 \qquad
 Z_i^{\sigma}=
 \begin{cases}
 R_i,&\sigma_i=R,\\
 D_i^{\parallel},&\sigma_i=\parallel,\\
 D_i^{\perp},&\sigma_i=\perp.
 \end{cases}
\]
The state vector $(R,\ldots,R)$ contains the reduced value together with the locally
created residual; every other state vector contributes propagation or interaction.

Let $\beta_v^{\chi}(\sigma)$ denote the operator norm of $\mu_v$ restricted to the
indicated components of its arguments and projected onto the component $\chi$ of its
output, for $\chi\in\{\parallel,\perp\}$. The $R$ and $\parallel$ states are carried by
the same projector but obey different magnitude bounds, which is why they are tracked
separately.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of the state-resolved recursion]
\label{thm:state}
Assume that for every internal vertex $v$ and every state vector
$\sigma\in\{R,\parallel,\perp\}^{a_v}$ an upper bound for $\beta_v^{\chi}(\sigma)$ is
available. Then the recursion that stores, at each vertex, upper bounds for
$\norm{F_v}$, $\norm{R_v}$, $\norm{D_v}$, $\norm{D_v^{\parallel}}$ and
$\norm{D_v^{\perp}}$, and combines them by
\[
 b_v^{\chi}
 \le\lambda_v^{\chi}
 +\sum_{\sigma\ne(R,\ldots,R)}\beta_v^{\chi}(\sigma)\prod_i b_{c_i}^{\sigma_i},
 \qquad
 \lambda_v^{\parallel}=0,
 \quad
 \lambda_v^{\perp}\le\rho_v\prod_ib_{c_i}^{R},
\]
returns valid upper bounds at the root. It performs $O\!\left(3^{a_v}\right)$ combination
steps at each vertex $v$, hence $O\!\left(\lvert T\rvert\,3^{a_{\max}}\right)$ in total.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:complexity}
The count in Theorem~\ref{thm:state} is a count of \emph{combination steps in the
recursion}. It excludes the cost of obtaining the bounds $\beta_v^{\chi}(\sigma)$, which
dominates in practice and which we do not analyse; see Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
\end{remark}

\section{Optimal ordering for multilinear telescoping}
\label{sec:order}

Comparing $\mu(F_1,\ldots,F_a)$ with $\mu(R_1,\ldots,R_a)$ by exchanging one argument at
a time produces a bound that depends on the order of exchange. Let $e_i\ge0$ bound the
error in slot $i$, let $r_i,f_i\ge0$ bound $\norm{R_i}$ and $\norm{F_i}$, let $G_i\ge0$ be
a slot gain, and put $w_i=G_ie_i$. For a permutation $\pi$ of $[a]$ the resulting bound is
\[
 C(\pi)=\sum_{t=1}^{a}w_{\pi_t}\prod_{s<t}r_{\pi_s}\prod_{s>t}f_{\pi_s}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Optimal order]
\label{thm:order}
Put $d_i=f_i-r_i$. Order the slots by placing those with $d_i>0$ first, then those with
$d_i=0$, then those with $d_i<0$, and sorting each of the two nonzero sign classes by
nondecreasing $w_i/d_i$. The resulting permutation minimises $C$ over all permutations.
Slots with $w_i=d_i=0$ may be placed anywhere.
\end{theorem}

The proof is an adjacent-exchange argument: placing $i$ before $j$ is no worse precisely
when $w_id_j\le w_jd_i$. What makes the argument yield a \emph{global} minimum, rather
than only a local one, is that this comparison is induced by the lexicographic key
$\bigl(\text{sign class of }d_i,\;w_i/d_i\bigr)$, with sign classes ordered positive,
zero, negative, and is therefore a total preorder; the details are in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:orderscope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:order} is optimal within the family of scalar telescoping bounds defined
by $C$. It says nothing about bounding strategies outside that family.
\end{remark}

\section{Uniform estimates}
\label{sec:uniform}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform coefficients]
\label{thm:homogeneous}
Suppose that $\norm{\mu_v}_\op\le M$ and $\norm{r_v}_\op\le\rho$ at every internal
vertex. Then for every typed composition tree with $k=k(T)\ge1$ internal vertices,
\[
 E_T^{\amb}\le k\rho M^{k-1}L_T,
 \qquad
 E_T^{\perp}\le k\rho M^{k-1}L_T,
\]
and
\[
 E_T^{\proj}=E_T^{\red}\le(k-1)\rho M^{k-1}L_T.
\]
\end{theorem}

The hypotheses are uniform over the vertices, but nothing else is assumed to be uniform:
types, arities, dimensions, ranks and the maps themselves may all differ from vertex to
vertex.

\begin{remark}[The case of a leaf]
\label{rem:leaf}
The statement is made for $k\ge1$. A leaf has $k=0$ and error $0$, and the expression
$M^{k-1}$ is not defined at $k=0$ when $M=0$; the induction in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} treats the leaf case separately, where the assertion reads
$0\le0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
Induction on the tree. At an internal vertex, multilinear telescoping over the children
together with the magnitude estimate $\norm{F_v},\norm{R_v}\le M^{k_v}L_v$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:magnitudes} bounds the propagated contribution by
$\bigl(\sum_ik_{c_i}\bigr)\rho M^{k_v-1}L_v=(k_v-1)\rho M^{k_v-1}L_v$. The locally created
residual adds at most $\rho\prod_i\norm{R_i}\le\rho M^{k_v-1}L_v$, which gives the ambient
estimate. For the projected estimate, $R\in\ran P$ gives $PF-R=PD_\varrho$, and
$Pr_\varrho=0$ removes the root residual, so only the propagated contribution survives.
Proposition~\ref{prop:root} identifies the reduced error with the projected one. The
orthogonal estimate follows from $(I-P)F=(I-P)D_\varrho$ and $\norm{I-P}=1$. Complete
details are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Where the difference of one comes from]
\label{rem:kminusone}
The reduction from $k$ to $k-1$ is not a refinement of the estimate: it is the exact
removal of one source. The residual created at the root lies in $(\ran P)^\perp$ and is
annihilated by the final projection. In the extreme case $k=1$, every child is a leaf, so
$D_\varrho=r_\varrho(R_1,\ldots,R_a)$ lies entirely in the orthogonal complement, and
\[
 E_T^{\proj}=E_T^{\red}=0
\]
\emph{exactly}, while $E_T^{\amb}$ need not vanish. The estimate is consistent:
$(k-1)\rho M^{k-1}L_T=0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Upper bound, not optimal constant]
\label{rem:notsharp}
Theorem~\ref{thm:homogeneous} is a chain of triangle inequalities. It establishes upper
bounds and nothing more. In particular it does not show that $k-1$ is the smallest
coefficient valid at a fixed value of $\eta=\rho/M>0$, nor for a fixed dimension,
projector rank, topology, or class of repeated maps. Sections~\ref{sec:lower}
and~\ref{sec:lowcases} report what is and is not known about this.
\end{remark}

\section{Pathwise estimates}
\label{sec:pathwise}

Fix a valid ordering at every vertex, so that the quantities $B_v$ produced by the
recursion of Section~\ref{sec:state} satisfy the affine scalar inequality
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:recurrence}
 B_v\le\lambda_v+\sum_{j}h_{v,j}B_{c_j},
 \qquad
 \lambda_v=\rho_v\prod_iB^{R}_{c_i},
 \qquad
 B_\ell=0,
\end{equation}
where $h_{v,j}\ge0$ is the gain for transporting an error from slot $j$ through $v$.

The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:recurrence} is an inequality, so $B_\varrho$ itself has no
closed form. We therefore introduce the quantity that does.

\begin{definition}[Pathwise majorant]
\label{def:majorant}
Let $\widehat B_v$ be defined by the \emph{equality}
\[
 \widehat B_\ell=0,
 \qquad
 \widehat B_v=\lambda_v+\sum_j h_{v,j}\widehat B_{c_j}
 \quad(v\in\Int T).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{corollary}[Pathwise attribution]
\label{cor:pathwise}
For every vertex $v$ one has $B_v\le\widehat B_v$, and at the root
\[
 \widehat B_{\varrho}
 =\sum_{v\in\Int T}\lambda_v
 \prod_{e\in\mathrm{path}(v,\varrho)}h_e ,
\]
the product being over the edges from $v$ to the root, each contributing the gain of the
slot through which it passes, and the product over the empty path being $1$. Consequently
\[
 B_\varrho\;\le\;\sum_{v\in\Int T}\lambda_v\prod_{e\in\mathrm{path}(v,\varrho)}h_e .
\]
For the projected error the root source is omitted, $\lambda_\varrho$ being replaced by
$0$, and the final edge carries a projected-output gain.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What the pathwise formula is, and is not]
\label{rem:pathwise}
Two distinctions matter here, and the earlier formulation blurred both.

First, the displayed formula is an \emph{identity for $\widehat B_\varrho$}, not for
$B_\varrho$. The recursion of Section~\ref{sec:state} produces upper bounds, so
\eqref{eq:recurrence} is an inequality; unrolling an inequality does not yield an equation.
Definition~\ref{def:majorant} supplies the majorant for which the unrolling is exact, and
the two statements of Corollary~\ref{cor:pathwise} are then separate: a comparison
$B_v\le\widehat B_v$, proved by induction, and a closed form for $\widehat B_\varrho$.

Second, even as a statement about $\widehat B$, the formula is not an expansion of the
multilinear cross terms of Theorem~\ref{thm:subset}. The interaction terms indexed by
subsets of size at least two were absorbed into the gains $h_{v,j}$ when
\eqref{eq:recurrence} was formed. Each summand therefore attributes a share of the
\emph{bound} to a vertex, not a share of the error.
\end{remark}

\section{Signed combinations of trees}
\label{sec:signed}

Let $\mathcal F=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}c_\alpha T_\alpha$ be a finite linear combination of
composition trees with compatible root types.

\begin{corollary}[Triangle bound]
\label{cor:signed}
$E_{\mathcal F}^{\proj}\le\sum_\alpha\lvert c_\alpha\rvert B_{T_\alpha}^{\proj}$. In
particular, the five-input ternary associator $\mu\circ_1\mu-\mu\circ_3\mu$, a difference
of two trees with two internal vertices each, satisfies $E^{\proj}\le2\rho ML$, whereas
the corresponding ambient bound is $4\rho ML$.
\end{corollary}

Combining syntactically identical trees before applying the triangle inequality is an
exact simplification, and it is the only unconditional cancellation available here. Any
further cancellation --- from shared subtrees, opposite signs, symmetry or antisymmetry
--- is not captured by Corollary~\ref{cor:signed}, and quantifying it is open
(Problem~\ref{prob:cancellation}).

\subsection{A quantity that vanishes numerically}

\section{Lower constructions}
\label{sec:lower}

An attained value is a rigorous lower bound only once admissibility has been verified.
The principal explicit family acts on a two-dimensional plane spanned by one direction in
$\ran P$ and one in its orthogonal complement. For $0\le\eta\le1$ put
\[
 G_\eta=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{1-\eta^2}&-\eta\\ \eta&\sqrt{1-\eta^2}\end{bmatrix}.
\]
The first argument is rotated by $G_\eta$ while the remaining arguments act through their
first coordinate. The multilinear operator norm of the resulting map is one and the norm
of its closure-residual map is exactly $\eta$. Embedding the plane isometrically into a
larger ambient space preserves the value.

Directed interval recursion in $160$-bit arithmetic encloses the normalised tree error of
this family. In a small number of low-dimensional cases an independent exact symbolic
routine enumerates the endpoints and stationary points on $[-1,1]$ and retains an exact
nonnegativity witness.

\section{Approximation of the maps}
\label{sec:representation}

Suppose each $\mu_v$ is replaced by an approximation $\widehat\mu_v$ with
$\norm{\mu_v-\widehat\mu_v}_\op\le\delta$, so that $\norm{\widehat\mu_v}_\op\le M+\delta$.
This models a low-rank or quantised representation of the maps, and it introduces an error
of a different kind from the closure residual. Write $F_\mu$, $R_\mu$ for the two
evaluations of the exact tree and $R_{\widehat\mu}$ for the recursively projected
evaluation of the approximate tree.

\begin{proposition}[Representation and projection]
\label{prop:approximation}
Under the standing hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:setting} for the exact maps
$\{\mu_v\}$, and with $\norm{\mu_v-\widehat\mu_v}_\op\le\delta$ at every internal vertex,
a tree with $k\ge1$ internal vertices satisfies
\[
 \norm{F_\mu-R_{\widehat\mu}}
 \le\underbrace{k\rho M^{k-1}L_T}_{\text{closure}}
 +\underbrace{k\delta(M+\delta)^{k-1}L_T}_{\text{representation}},
\]
and
\[
 \norm{PF_\mu-R_{\widehat\mu}}
 \le\underbrace{(k-1)\rho M^{k-1}L_T}_{\text{closure}}
 +\underbrace{k\delta(M+\delta)^{k-1}L_T}_{\text{representation}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[No closure hypothesis is imposed on the approximate maps]
\label{rem:noapproxclosure}
The bound is obtained by inserting $R_\mu$, not $F_{\widehat\mu}$:
\[
 \norm{F_\mu-R_{\widehat\mu}}
 \le\norm{F_\mu-R_\mu}+\norm{R_\mu-R_{\widehat\mu}}.
\]
The first term is Theorem~\ref{thm:homogeneous} applied to the \emph{exact} tree, and the
second compares two recursively projected evaluations, which differ only in the maps. The
alternative route --- inserting the unprojected approximate tree $F_{\widehat\mu}$ and
applying Theorem~\ref{thm:homogeneous} to it --- would require a bound on
$\norm{(I-P_v)\widehat\mu_v(P\,\cdot,\ldots,P\,\cdot)}_\op$, that is, a closure hypothesis
on the \emph{approximate} maps. No such hypothesis is available: $\delta$ bounds the
distance between $\mu_v$ and $\widehat\mu_v$ and says nothing about how $\widehat\mu_v$
interacts with the prescribed subspaces. The route taken here uses only hypotheses that
have been declared.
\end{remark}

The two displayed terms are an algebraic partition of one valid upper bound. They are not a
decomposition of the observed error, and no equality with it is asserted. In particular the
reconstruction error of a low-rank representation is not folded into $\rho$, and no closure
property is attributed to the approximation.

\section{Negative controls}
\label{sec:controls}

The hypotheses are operational rather than decorative, and each is tested by a control
that fails when it is dropped.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item If the projector is oblique, Proposition~\ref{prop:root} fails
(Example~\ref{ex:oblique}).
\item A type-invalid edge cannot be repaired by numerical broadcasting. All $48$ declared
invalid configurations are rejected before evaluation.
\item The projected constant of a single-vertex tree is exactly zero, which refutes any
formula that assigns the locally created residual to the projected error of the vertex
that creates it.
\item Cancellation within a signed combination can make the triangle bound strict; a
numerical lower bound never reverses the direction of an upper bound.
\item A larger ambient space contains the two-dimensional construction isometrically, but
that embedding alone gives no evidence that additional coordinates improve the constant.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The exact constants at fixed $\eta>0$ are unknown for most projected, typed,
repeated-map and signed-combination families.
\item Interval enclosures certify the value of an explicit construction. They do not
certify a supremum unless a matching upper bound closes the gap.
\item The specified optimiser grid was not executed in full, under a stated
computational-cost constraint. The executed portion is reported as such.
\item Multilinear operator norms are computed exactly only for the declared explicit
constructions. For general dense and low-rank maps a Frobenius upper bound is substituted,
which inflates $M$ and therefore every constant that depends on it.
\item The complexity statement in Theorem~\ref{thm:state} counts recursion steps and
excludes the cost of obtaining the block norms it assumes.
\item All results are finite-dimensional. No statement is made about continuum limits,
infinite trees, measurability, or the behaviour of these estimates under discretisation.
\item Neither the proofs nor the numerical results have been checked by anyone other than
the author, and no assessment of originality has been carried out
(Remark~\ref{rem:originality}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open problems}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{problem}[Exact projected constant]
\label{prob:constant}
Determine $C_T^{\proj}(\eta)$ for nontrivial families of topologies. Deciding between
$C_T^{\proj}(\eta)=k-1$ for all admissible $\eta$; $C_T^{\proj}(\eta)<k-1$ for $\eta>0$
with $\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}C_T^{\proj}(\eta)=k-1$; and
$C_T^{\proj}(\eta)\le\Gamma_T(\eta)<k-1$ for some smaller function $\Gamma_T$, would each
be a substantive result. Nothing in this article favours one over the others. The case
$k=2$ should be settled first.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Dimension and rank]
\label{prob:dimension}
Decide whether ambient dimension or projector rank can improve on the isometrically
embedded planar constructions of Section~\ref{sec:lower}.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Cancellation]
\label{prob:cancellation}
Obtain rigorous constants for the associator, the Filippov identity and the Jacobi
combination that account for cancellation between terms, rather than bounding each term
separately.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Symbolic status of the six-term expression]
\label{prob:gji}
Decide, by exact expansion in the free operad, whether the declared six-term expression of
Section~\ref{sec:gji} is identically zero.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Comparison of the estimates]
\label{prob:domination}
Characterise when the state-resolved, pathwise and order-optimised estimates dominate one
another. The ratios in Table~\ref{tab:hierarchy} are medians over configurations and do
not establish an ordering.
\end{problem}

\begin{problem}[Operator norms]
\label{prob:norms}
Replace the Frobenius upper bounds by validated enclosures of the multilinear spectral
norm at a useful scale.
\end{problem}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Recursive orthogonal projection on a finite typed multilinear tree admits an exact local
error decomposition. From it follow a sound state-resolved recursion, a closed-form
optimal ordering within a declared scalar family, a pathwise attribution of the bound to
individual vertices, and the uniform distinction between $k$ ambient sources and $k-1$
projected sources. The last of these is an upper bound whose optimality is open already
for two internal vertices, and the numerical evidence assembled here is broad enough to
expose the dependence on topology, interaction, precision and representation without
resolving it.
