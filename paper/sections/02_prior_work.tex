\section{Prior work and positioning}

The framework touches non-associative algebra, ternary and Filippov algebras, operadic compositions, CP tensor decompositions, invariant-subspace reduction, kernel operators, and discrete Hodge theory. Filippov's original $n$-Lie framework is a reference point for skew-symmetric brackets and the fundamental identity \cite{Filippov1985}; Akivis and Sabinin structures show that commutators and associators sit inside a broader non-associative Lie theory \cite{Sabinin1999,MostovoyPerezIzquierdoShestakov2014}. Operadic composition provides the appropriate language for polynomial identities rather than a new terminology for them \cite{LodayVallette2012}.

The tensor component is positioned against CP/Tucker/HOSVD practice and factor identifiability, not presented as a new decomposition \cite{KoldaBader2009,BaderKolda2007,Kruskal1977}. The reduction component is adjacent to projection-based and structure-preserving model reduction \cite{BennerGugercinWillcox2015,ChaturantabutBeattieGugercin2016}; any SEION-specific claim therefore needs an explicit theorem or a measurable guarantee beyond choosing a projector. Spectral snapping is a finite thresholding operation whose perturbation analysis is naturally compared with Davis--Kahan subspace perturbation theory \cite{DavisKahan1970}. The finite cohomological track is deliberately weaker than continuum Hodge theory and is positioned against finite-difference/discrete approximations \cite{Dodziuk1976}.

The repository's novelty registry avoids renaming a known construction as a new theorem. Its current limited new component is organizational: a declared law, a declared defect convention, a reduction diagnostic, and a reproducible artifact are joined in one traceable path. This is a software/reproducibility contribution until a stronger mathematical theorem is proved.

The prior-art matrix is generated from \texttt{claims/novelty\_registry.yaml}. No priority claim is made for the classical identities or for CP gauge non-uniqueness.
