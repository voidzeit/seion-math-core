## 3. Their setting and assumptions

- T = P_{M_2}P_{M_1}, product of TWO orthogonal projections on a (separable, complex) Hilbert space.
- Objects: numerical range W(T), numerical radius, spectrum; Halmos two-subspace theorem reduces to 2×2 blocks.

## 4. Their main results relevant here

- Theorem 1.2: closure of W(T) is the closed convex hull of elliptical discs with foci 0 and λ and minor axis
  √(λ(1−λ)), λ ranging over the spectrum of T. Theorem 1.3: without closure, over eigenvalues, when
  P_{M_1}P_{M_2}P_{M_1} is diagonalizable.
- Proposition 1.4: numerical radius equals (r(T) + √r(T))/2.
- Proposition 1.5: W(T) lies in a sector with vertex 1 whose half-opening is arctan of an explicit function
  of the Friedrichs cosine (refining Crouzeix's π/6-type sector result for two projections).
- Applications to alternating projections and to annihilating pairs (uncertainty principle).
