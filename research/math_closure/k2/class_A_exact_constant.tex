\section*{Exact normalized constant for the declared $k=2$ general class}
\label{sec:k2-class-a-exact}

Let $\mathcal A_2$ be the declared finite-dimensional real binary-chain class:
orthogonal proper projectors, unit leaves, arbitrary bounded bilinear laws at
the two nodes (shared or independent), and uniform bounds $M$ and $\rho$ with
$\eta=\rho/M\in(0,1]$. Define $C_{2,\mathcal A}^P(\eta)$ as the supremum
of $E_{\mathrm{proj}}/(\rho M)$ over this class.

\textbf{Theorem.}
\[
  \boxed{C_{2,\mathcal A}^P(\eta)=1\qquad(0<\eta\le1).}
\]

\textbf{Proof.} The universal projected-root theorem gives
$E_{\mathrm{proj}}\le\rho M$ for every admissible configuration, hence
$C_{2,\mathcal A}^P(\eta)\le1$. For the reverse inequality, use a
two-dimensional rank-one witness with $P=\operatorname{proj}_{\operatorname{span}\{e_0\}}$
and
\[
  \mu(x,y)=M x_1y_0e_0+\rho x_0y_0e_1.
\]
Its operator norm is $M$ and its closure defect is $\rho$ whenever
$0<\rho\le M$. With all unit leaves equal to $e_0$, the inner ambient value
is $\rho e_1$ while the recursively projected inner value is zero. The outer
ambient evaluation is therefore $\rho M e_0$, whereas the projected
evaluation is zero. Thus $E_{\mathrm{proj}}=\rho M$, proving
$C_{2,\mathcal A}^P(\eta)\ge1$. The witness embeds isometrically into every
larger finite dimension and is therefore admissible in the declared class.
\hfill$\square$

\textbf{Scope.} This closes the declared finite-dimensional binary class.
It does not assert a higher-arity $k=2$ result, an infinite-dimensional
attainment statement, or any novelty or independent-review conclusion.
