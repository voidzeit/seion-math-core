\section*{Asymptotic $(k-1)$ sharpness for every finite binary tree}
\label{sec:m18-binary-tree-asymptotic}

Let $T$ be any finite ordered full-binary tree with $k\ge1$ internal
vertices.  Work on the real plane $\mathbb R^2$ identified with $\mathbb C$, with
$P(z)=\operatorname{Re}(z)$, unit leaves equal to $1$, and put
$\theta=\arcsin\eta$, $c=\cos\theta$.  At every internal vertex other than
the root use the independently selectable law
\[
  \mu_\theta(z,w)=e^{i\theta}zw,
\]
where complex multiplication is the Euclidean norm-one bilinear law.  At
the root use instead
\[
  \mu_\star(z,w)=\bigl(\operatorname{Im}z\operatorname{Re}w
  +\operatorname{Re}z\operatorname{Im}w\bigr)\,1.
\]
Both laws have operator norm one; the non-root closure defect is exactly
$\sin\theta=\eta$, while the root closure defect is zero.

\textbf{Theorem.}  For this witness,
\[
  E_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(\eta)
  =\left|\sin\bigl((k-1)\theta\bigr)\right|
  =\left|\sin\bigl((k-1)\arcsin\eta\bigr)\right|.
\]
Consequently, for the independent-law extremal constant,
\[
  \boxed{
  \lim_{\eta\downarrow0} C_{T,\mathrm{ind}}^P(\eta)=k-1.
  }
\]

\textbf{Proof.}  For every subtree $S$ rooted at a non-root internal vertex,
complex multiplication and the one rotation at each internal vertex give the
ambient value $F_S=e^{i k(S)\theta}$, independently of the shape of $S$.
The recursively projected value is $R_S=c^{k(S)}$.  If the root children
contain $k_L$ and $k_R$ internal vertices, then $k_L+k_R=k-1$ and the root
ambient value is
\[
 \mu_\star(e^{ik_L\theta},e^{ik_R\theta})
 =\sin(k_L\theta)\cos(k_R\theta)
  +\cos(k_L\theta)\sin(k_R\theta)
 =\sin((k-1)\theta).
\]
The root's recursively projected inputs are real, so $\mu_\star$ returns
zero in the projected evaluation.  This proves the exact witness formula.
The universal projected-root theorem gives
$C_{T,\mathrm{ind}}^P(\eta)\le k-1$ for every $\eta$.  Dividing the witness
formula by $\eta$ and using
$\sin((k-1)\arcsin\eta)/\eta\to k-1$ gives the matching lower limit.
\hfill$\square$

For a general operator scale $M$, multiply every internal law by $M$; the
error and denominator both scale by $M^k$.  The theorem is asymptotic in
$\eta$ and does not assert fixed-$\eta$ equality, nor does it cover variable
arity or growing-tree limits.
