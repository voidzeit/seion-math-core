\section*{Exact fixed-$\eta$ $k=3$ constant for every finite arity profile}
\label{sec:m20-k3-arbitrary-arity}

Consider a finite ordered rooted tree with exactly three internal vertices,
where every internal arity is at least two.  We first record the reduction
used below.  If a node law is
$\mu:\prod_{j=1}^a V_j\to V$ and the leaf slots are frozen at unit vectors
$\ell_j\in\operatorname{Ran}P_j$, define the effective law on the internal
slots $I$ by
\[
 \widetilde\mu((x_i)_{i\in I})
 :=\mu(y_1,\ldots,y_a),\qquad
 y_j=\begin{cases}x_j,&j\in I,\\\ell_j,&j\notin I.
 \end{cases}
\]
Multilinearity and $\|\ell_j\|=1$ give
$\|\widetilde\mu\|_{\rm op}\le\|\mu\|_{\rm op}$.  Because the frozen
leaf slots are already in their projected ranges, the same substitution in
the local closure map gives
\[
 \|(I-P)\widetilde\mu(P_i\cdot)_{i\in I}\|_{\rm op}
 \le \|(I-P)\mu(P_1\cdot,\ldots,P_a\cdot)\|_{\rm op}.
\]
Thus freezing unit projected leaves preserves admissibility budgets.  The
resulting effective map is linear if there is one internal child and bilinear
if there are two.  Since three internal vertices permit only the
internal-child skeletons
\[
  \text{chain}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\text{branching},
\]
M13--M15 give the universal upper bound $W_3(\eta)$ for every finite arity
profile.

Conversely, take the dimension-two rank-one witnesses from M14 or M15 and
insert a unit $e_0$ gate in every additional leaf slot.  The resulting
multilinear law has the same operator norm and closure residual as the
effective linear or bilinear law.  Therefore the witness embeds into every
allowed arity profile and attains the same value.

\textbf{Theorem.}  For every finite ordered rooted tree $T$ with exactly three
internal vertices and all internal arities at least two,
\[
  \boxed{C_{T,\mathrm{ind}}^P(\eta)=W_3(\eta)}
  \qquad (0<\eta\le1),
\]
where
\[
 W_3(\eta)=
 \begin{cases}
 \sqrt{4-3\eta^2},&0<\eta\le\sqrt{2/3},\\[1mm]
 2/(\sqrt3\,\eta),&\sqrt{2/3}\le\eta\le1.
 \end{cases}
\]

The theorem is a fixed-$k=3$ independent-law result.  It does not close
same-law/gated restrictions, arbitrary fixed-$\eta$ constants for $k\ge4$,
or uniform growing-tree questions.
\hfill$\square$
