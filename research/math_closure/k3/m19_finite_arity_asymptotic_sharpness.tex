\section*{Asymptotic $(k-1)$ sharpness for every finite arity-compatible tree}
\label{sec:m19-finite-arity-asymptotic}

Let $T$ be any finite ordered rooted tree with arity at least two at each
internal vertex, and let $k$ be its number of internal vertices.  Identify the
real plane with $\mathbb C$, take $P(z)=\operatorname{Re}(z)$, unit leaves,
and set $\theta=\arcsin\eta$.  At every non-root vertex of arity $m$ use
\[
  \mu_{m,\theta}(z_1,\ldots,z_m)=e^{i\theta}z_1\cdots z_m,
\]
viewed as a real $m$-linear map on $\mathbb R^2\cong\mathbb C$.  At the root,
of arity $d$, use
\[
  \mu_{\star,d}(z_1,\ldots,z_d)=
  \operatorname{Im}(z_1\cdots z_d)\,1.
\]
Both maps have operator norm one.  Every non-root projected input is real, so
the local non-root closure defect is $\sin\theta=\eta$, while the root defect
is zero.

\textbf{Theorem.}  The witness has
\[
  E_T^{\mathrm{proj}}(\eta)=
  \left|\sin\bigl((k-1)\theta\bigr)\right|
  =\left|\sin\bigl((k-1)\arcsin\eta\bigr)\right|.
\]
Consequently, the universal projected-root coefficient is asymptotically
sharp for every fixed finite arity-compatible topology:
\[
  \boxed{\displaystyle
  \lim_{\eta\downarrow0}C_{T,\mathrm{ind}}^P(\eta)=k-1.}
\]

\textbf{Proof.}  For a subtree $S$ rooted at a non-root internal vertex,
real-plane multiplication gives the ambient value
$F_S=e^{ik(S)\theta}$, independently of both its shape and the arities of
its vertices.  Recursive projection gives $R_S=\cos(\theta)^{k(S)}$.
If the root children have internal-node counts $k_1,\ldots,k_d$, then
$\sum_j k_j=k-1$ and the ambient root output is
\[
 \operatorname{Im}\prod_{j=1}^d e^{ik_j\theta}
 =\sin((k-1)\theta).
\]
All recursively projected child outputs are real, so the projected root
output is zero.  This proves the witness formula.  The universal projected
bound supplies the upper limit $k-1$, and division by $\eta$ gives the stated
limit.
\hfill$\square$

The result is asymptotic in $\eta$ and does not assert fixed-$\eta$ equality,
same-law/gated sharpness, or uniformity for growing trees.
