\section*{M10 endpoint addendum: strict gap at $\eta=1$}
\label{sec:m10-endpoint-eta-one}

\textbf{Historical-scope correction.} This addendum preserves the original
compactness route as provenance. The M10 equality obstruction used below is a
chain argument and should not be read as a branching proof. The current
endpoint result for both topologies is stronger and is supplied by M14--M15:
\[
 C_{3,\mathrm{ind}}^{P,\mathrm{chain}}(1)
 =C_{3,\mathrm{ind}}^{P,\mathrm{branch}}(1)
 =\frac{2}{\sqrt3}<\sqrt2=U_3(1).
\]

\paragraph{Statement.}
In the independent-law binary $k=3$ chain and branching classes, the M9
envelope at the endpoint is
\[
  U_3(1)=\sqrt{2}.
\]
No individual admissible configuration attains this value, and the fixed-tree
support-compression theorem reduces the endpoint supremum to a finite union
of compact classes.  Consequently
\[
  C_{3,\mathrm{ind}}^P(1)<\sqrt{2}.
\]

\paragraph{Proof.}
At $\eta=1$ one has $\rho=M$.  The M9 scalar envelope is
\[
 h(q)=M^2q+M\rho\sqrt{M^2-q^2},
 \qquad 0\le q\le\rho,
\]
whose unique maximizer is the interior point
\[
 q^*=\frac{M}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad h(q^*)=\sqrt2\,M^3.
\]
Both equality contributions are strictly positive:
\[
 M^2q^*=\frac{M^3}{\sqrt2}>0,
 \qquad
 M\rho\sqrt{M^2-(q^*)^2}=\frac{M^3}{\sqrt2}>0.
\]
Therefore equality in the M9 envelope would require the same three
conditions used in M10: exact operator-norm saturation on the first
propagated direction at the second node, exact operator-norm saturation on
the corresponding root direction, and equality in the final triangle
inequality for both nonzero propagated contributions.  M10's
projector-independent Step 1 shows that the second propagated contribution
cannot be a nonzero scalar multiple of the first.  Its Step 2 then shows that
the simultaneous root saturation and triangle equality are impossible.  The
argument only requires $q^*>0$ and a nonzero second contribution; it does not
require $\rho<M$.  Hence no individual configuration attains $U_3(1)$.

Finally, fixed-tree support compression supplies a uniform finite support
dimension bound for these two fixed topologies.  The admissible tensor,
projector, and unit-leaf parameter sets in the resulting finite union are
compact, and the projected error is continuous.  The supremum is therefore a
maximum in that compact union.  Since no point attains $\sqrt2$, the maximum
is strictly smaller than $\sqrt2$.  The gap is not quantified and the exact
endpoint constant remains open. $\square$
