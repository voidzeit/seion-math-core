\begin{abstract}
We study the worst-case discrepancy between ambient evaluation and recursive orthogonal projection on a finite typed tree of multilinear laws, with local law norm at most \(M\), closure residual at most \(\rho\), and \(k\) internal vertices.  An exact child-error subset expansion yields the universal bounds \(E^{\rm amb}\le k\rho M^{k-1}L\) and \(E^P=E^{\rm red}\le(k-1)\rho M^{k-1}L\), together with mixed-block dynamic-programming and residual path-sum certificates.  A pair-exchange argument gives the globally optimal slot order within the declared scalar telescoping family.  A gated planar-rotation construction matches the ambient coefficient in registered cells, but does not establish fixed-positive-\(\eta=\rho/M\) sharpness of the projected coefficient \(k-1\).  Directed interval calculations certify \VThreeExactCells{} small cells globally; across the full gap registry the maximum unresolved absolute and relative gaps are \VThreeMaxAbsoluteGap{} and \VThreeMaxRelativeGap{}.  The finite base study contains \VThreeScientificInstances{} scientific instances, \VThreeTreeOccurrences{} enumerated tree occurrences, and \VThreeLeakageMasks{} exhaustive leakage masks, with CPU/GPU difference at most \VThreeMaxParity{}.  The main limitations are that theorem-level novelty is not established, the extended adversarial grid is resource-gated, and most fixed-\(\eta\) constants remain open; the release status is therefore \VThreeReleaseStatus{}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Finite compositions of multilinear operations occur in nonassociative
identities, colored-operad syntax, hierarchical tensor calculations, and
structure-preserving reduction.  A common computational operation is to
project the output of every internal node back to a selected subspace.  Even
when every local projection defect is small, nested composition can amplify
or cancel those defects in a topology-dependent way.  A global norm product
is safe but can obscure the source, direction, and interaction order of the
error.

This paper fixes a finite typed ordered tree and asks a precise optimization
question: among laws, compatible orthogonal projectors, and unit reduced leaf
data satisfying declared local norm and closure constraints, what is the best
constant controlling ambient, projected-root, normal-root, or reduced-coordinate
error?  We separate four logically distinct tasks: proving universal upper
bounds, constructing admissible lower examples, certifying small global
optima, and searching larger instances empirically.  The last task is never
used as a proof.

The strongest universal result is the difference between \(k\) and \(k-1\).
The ambient and normal errors may include a new residual at the root, whereas
the projected and reduced root errors cannot.  This removes exactly one
source in a homogeneous tree.  The coefficient is a theorem-level upper
bound; its fixed-\(\eta\) optimality is not inferred from the form of the
proof.

\section{Contributions and epistemic status}

The contributions are mapped one-to-one to registry entries and evidence:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item an exact local subset expansion and exact orthogonal relationship among
the four named root errors (Theorem~\ref{thm:subset} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:root});
\item universal \(k\) and \(k-1\) homogeneous coefficients valid for
heterogeneous types, laws, and arities under uniform \(M,\rho\)
(Theorem~\ref{thm:homogeneous});
\item a mixed-state dynamic program, residual path sums, and a globally
optimal ordering theorem within the scalar telescoping family
(Theorems~\ref{thm:dp} and~\ref{thm:order});
\item certified explicit lower constructions, exact small symbolic cases,
and independent empirical searches with explicit optimality gaps; and
\item a deduplicated finite experiment and artifact protocol that tests sound
bounds, topology effects, signed forests, CP/projection separation, precision,
and CPU/GPU parity.
\end{enumerate}

The exact expansion and ambient coefficient are conservative specializations
of standard multilinear analysis.  The projected-root, mixed-mask, and
path-sum formulations were not located verbatim in the bounded prior-art
search, but absence from that search is not novelty evidence.  Every candidate
novelty claim therefore remains \texttt{NOVELTY\_NOT\_ESTABLISHED} and every
theorem remains \texttt{PENDING\_HUMAN\_REVIEW}.

\section{Prior art}

Typed tree composition is standard in colored operads
\cite{Yau2016,LodayVallette2012}.  Filippov's (n)-Lie identity and the
Akivis--Sabinin landscape motivate signed nonassociative forests
\cite{Filippov1985,MostovoyPerezIzquierdoShestakov2014}.  Approximate
multiplicativity in Banach algebras studies stability of maps under different
global hypotheses \cite{Johnson1988}; it is adjacent to, but does not directly
solve, the fixed-tree recursive projection problem.

Forward and backward error analysis already make composition order and local
perturbation propagation central \cite{Higham2002}.  Layered Lipschitz
certificates and sound abstract interpretation likewise demonstrate that
architecture-aware summaries can improve naive products
\cite{CombettesPesquet2020,GehrEtAl2018}.  These are strong prior-art reasons
not to claim that a dynamic program is intrinsically new.

Hierarchical tensor formats use dimension trees and topology-adaptive
approximation \cite{HackbuschKuhn2009,BallaniGrasedyck2014}.  Their principal
error is representation or rank truncation, while the present principal error
is a normal closure defect created at every algebraic node.  CP approximation,
factor nonuniqueness, and identifiability are kept separate
\cite{KoldaBader2009,Kruskal1977}.  Projection-based structure-preserving
model reduction supplies further adjacent precedent \cite{EggerEtAl2018}.

Validated interval arithmetic and moment/SOS methods provide global
certification tools when their hypotheses and solver statuses are verified
\cite{MooreKearfottCloud2009,Lasserre2001}.  V3 uses directed interval
recursion and exact symbolic elimination in selected low-dimensional cases;
it does not label an imported SDP interface as a certificate merely because
the package imports.

\section{Typed composition trees}

Let \(\Types\) be a finite set of types.  For each \(\tau\in\Types\), let
\(V_\tau\) and \(W_\tau\) be finite-dimensional real or complex Hilbert
spaces, let \(Q_\tau:W_\tau\to V_\tau\) be an isometry, and put
\(P_\tau=Q_\tau Q_\tau^*\).  An internal vertex \(v\) of arity \(a_v\)
carries a bounded law

\[
 \mu_v:\prod_{j=1}^{a_v}V_{\tau(v,j)}\longrightarrow V_{\tau(v)}.
\]

Each ordered child edge must match its declared input type.  Type-invalid
trees have no numerical semantics and are rejected before contraction.

\section{Ambient and recursively projected evaluations}

For reduced leaf data \(z_\ell\in W_{\tau(\ell)}\), define

\[
 F_\ell=R_\ell=Q_{\tau(\ell)}z_\ell,
 \quad
 F_v=\mu_v(F_{c_1},\ldots,F_{c_{a_v}}),
 \quad
 R_v=P_{\tau(v)}\mu_v(R_{c_1},\ldots,R_{c_{a_v}}).
\]

At the root, write \(F=F_T\), \(R=R_T\), and \(P=P_{\tau(T)}\).  The four
errors are

\[
 E_T^{\rm amb}=\norm{F-R},\quad
 E_T^P=\norm{PF-R},\quad
 E_T^N=\norm{(I-P)F},\quad
 E_T^{\rm red}=\norm{Q^*F-Q^*R}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exact root geometry]
\label{prop:root}
For every valid typed tree,

\[
 (E_T^{\rm amb})^2=(E_T^P)^2+(E_T^N)^2,
 \qquad E_T^{\rm red}=E_T^P.
\]
\end{proposition}

The proof is the orthogonal decomposition
\(F-R=(PF-R)+(I-P)F\); Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} records the complete
argument.  Orthogonality is essential and is tested independently in the
reference and vectorized evaluators.

\section{Exact local error decomposition}

At a node \(v\), abbreviate \(F_i=F_{c_i}\), \(R_i=R_{c_i}\), and
\(D_i=F_i-R_i\).  Define

\[
 r_v=(I-P_v)\mu_v(P_{c_1}\cdot,\ldots,P_{c_a}\cdot).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Exact child-error subset expansion]
\label{thm:subset}
For \(y_i^S=D_i\) if \(i\in S\) and \(y_i^S=R_i\) otherwise,

\[
 D_v=r_v(R_1,\ldots,R_a)
 +\sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[a]}
 \mu_v(y_1^S,\ldots,y_a^S).
\]
\end{theorem}

The term indexed by \(S\) records exactly which child branches carry an
error.  Singleton subsets are first-order propagated errors; larger subsets
are genuine branch interactions.  Applying \(P_v\) removes the local
residual exactly but not the propagated interactions.

\section{Dynamic-programming certificates}

For each node, the implementation stores \(B_v^F,B_v^R,B_v^A,B_v^P,B_v^N\)
and certified mixed-block norms.  Splitting each child error as
\(D_i=D_i^P+D_i^N\) creates three slot states: recursively projected \(R\),
projected error \(D^P\), and normal error \(D^N\).  Every state vector other
than all \(R\) is bounded separately.

\begin{theorem}[Sound mixed-mask recursion]
\label{thm:dp}
The three-state recursion returns valid full, projected, and normal root
certificates.  Its complexity is

\[
 O\!\left(|T|3^{a_{\max}}+|T|a_{\max}\log a_{\max}\right),
\]

and is linear in \(|T|\) for the declared arities two through four.
\end{theorem}

\section{Optimal telescoping order}

For a single multilinear replacement, let \(e_i\) bound the slot error,
\(r_i,f_i\) the projected and ambient child magnitudes, and \(G_i\) a
slot-specific gain.  Set \(w_i=G_ie_i\) and

\[
 C(\pi)=\sum_t w_{\pi_t}
 \prod_{s<t}r_{\pi_s}\prod_{s>t}f_{\pi_s}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Globally optimal order within the scalar family]
\label{thm:order}
Put \(d_i=f_i-r_i\).  Sort positive \(d_i\), then zero \(d_i\), then
negative \(d_i\), and within each nonzero sign class sort \(w_i/d_i\)
increasingly.  The resulting permutation globally minimizes \(C(\pi)\).
\end{theorem}

The adjacent exchange condition is
\(w_i(f_j-r_j)\le w_j(f_i-r_i)\).  Exhaustive property tests compare the
sorting rule against every permutation through arity seven, including zero
and negative denominators.  The theorem optimizes this scalar telescoping
family only.

\section{Homogeneous universal bounds}

Let \(k(T)\) be the number of internal vertices and
\(L_T=\prod_\ell\norm{z_\ell}\).  Assume every node law has norm at most
\(M\) and every closure map has norm at most \(\rho\).

\begin{theorem}[Universal coefficients]
\label{thm:homogeneous}
For every finite valid typed tree,

\[
 E_T^{\rm amb}\le k\rho M^{k-1}L_T,
 \qquad
 E_T^N\le k\rho M^{k-1}L_T,
\]

and

\[
 E_T^P=E_T^{\rm red}\le(k-1)\rho M^{k-1}L_T.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof is an induction on the tree.  Propagated child errors contribute
\(\sum_i k_{c_i}=k-1\), and the new root residual contributes one additional
unit only to ambient and normal error.

\section{Projected-root improvement}

The coefficient \(k-1\) is not a heuristic correction.  The root local
residual belongs to the normal space and is annihilated by \(P\).  In
particular, a one-node tree has \(E_T^P=E_T^{\rm red}=0\), even when its
ambient error is nonzero.  For a five-input ternary associator represented
as two signed two-node trees, the immediate triangle coefficient is therefore
\(1+1=2\), not the ambient \(2+2=4\).

This proof establishes a universal upper.  It does not establish that \(k-1\)
is the best coefficient at every fixed \(\eta=\rho/M>0\), dimension, rank,
topology, or repeated-law class.

\section{Nodewise path-sum bounds}

Fix a valid telescoping order at each ancestor.  If a source residual at
node \(v\) has magnitude bound
\(\lambda_v=\rho_v\prod_iB_{c_i}^R\), and \(h_{a,j}\) is the certified gain
for propagating child \(j\) through ancestor \(a\), then

\[
 B_{T,\rm path}^{\rm amb}
 =\sum_{v\in\Int T}\lambda_v
 \prod_{(a,j)\in\operatorname{path}(v,\operatorname{root})}h_{a,j}.
\]

For projected-root error, the root source is absent and the final edge uses
a projected-output gain.  The artifact schema records every source
contribution so that the total can be independently reconstructed.

\section{Sharpness constructions}

The principal explicit family acts on a two-dimensional tangent/normal plane.
For \(0\le\eta\le1\), let

\[
 T_\eta=\begin{bmatrix}
 \sqrt{1-\eta^2}&-\eta\\
 \eta&\sqrt{1-\eta^2}
 \end{bmatrix}.
\]

The first slot is rotated by \(T_\eta\), while all remaining slots gate on
their first coordinate.  The multilinear operator norm is one and the local
closure norm is exactly \(\eta\).  Embedding this plane into larger ambient
dimensions preserves the lower value.  Directed interval recursion encloses
the resulting normalized tree error.

\section{Counterexamples and negative controls}

The assumptions are operational, not decorative.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item If \(P\) is oblique, Proposition~\ref{prop:root} can fail.  For
\(P=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\) and \(D=(0,1)^\top\), the
components \(PD=(1,0)^\top\) and \((I-P)D=(-1,1)^\top\) are not orthogonal.
\item A type-invalid edge cannot be repaired by numerical broadcasting; all
48 declared invalid block-E controls are rejected before evaluation.
\item A one-node projected constant is exactly zero, disproving any formula
that blindly assigns one local residual to projected-root error.
\item Cancellation in a signed forest can make a triangle certificate strict;
an empirical lower never reverses the upper-bound implication.
\item Larger dimension contains the dimension-two construction, but this
embedding alone does not show that additional coordinates improve the optimum.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Approximation plus projection}

Let \(\widehat\mu_v\) approximate \(\mu_v\) with
\(\delta_v=\norm{\mu_v-\widehat\mu_v}_{\op}\).  Inserting the ambient
\(\widehat\mu\)-tree separates representation from recursive projection:

\[
 \norm{F_\mu-R_{\widehat\mu}}
 \le\norm{F_\mu-F_{\widehat\mu}}
    +\norm{F_{\widehat\mu}-R_{\widehat\mu}}.
\]

In the homogeneous \(k\)-node case, the transparent budget is

\begin{align*}
 E_{\rm repr}&\le k\delta(M+\delta)^{k-1}L,\\
 E_{\rm closure,base}&\le c\rho M^{k-1}L,\\
 E_{\rm interaction}&\le
 c\rho\bigl[(M+\delta)^{k-1}-M^{k-1}\bigr]L,
\end{align*}

with \(c=k\) for ambient error and \(c=k-1\) for projected-root error.
CP reconstruction error is not folded into \(\rho\).

\section{Limitations}

The principal limitations are mathematical rather than cosmetic.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The fixed-\(\eta\) optimal constants are unknown for most projected,
typed, repeated-law, and signed-forest families.
\item The exact \(k-1\) formulation and mixed-mask specialization have not
received an independent expert novelty review.
\item Interval enclosures certify explicit constructions, not the global
supremum unless an independent upper closes the gap.
\item The base matrix retains every requested scientific axis, but the full
seed/restart optimizer grid is pending under an explicit resource gate.
\item Operator norms are exact only for declared special constructions;
general dense and CP laws may use Frobenius upper bounds.
\item All results are finite-dimensional.  No continuum, infinite-tree,
measurability, or discretization-limit theorem is claimed.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open problems}

The central open questions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item determine the exact fixed-\(\eta\) projected constant
\(C_T^P(\eta)\) for nontrivial topology families;
\item decide whether dimension or projector rank can improve upon embedded
planar constructions;
\item obtain cancellation-aware certified constants for associator, FI, GJI,
and Jacobiator forests;
\item characterize when mixed-mask, path-sum, and optimized telescoping
certificates dominate one another;

\section{Conclusion}

Recursive orthogonal projection on a typed multilinear tree has an exact local
error algebra.  That algebra yields a sound source-resolved dynamic program,
a closed-form optimal ordering within the scalar telescoping family, and the
universal distinction between \(k\) ambient sources and \(k-1\) projected
sources.  The finite evidence is broad enough to expose topology,
interaction, precision, and representation effects, but not broad enough to
resolve most fixed-\(\eta\) optima or theorem-level novelty.  The appropriate
outcome is therefore a reproducible research draft with a fail-closed release
gate.
